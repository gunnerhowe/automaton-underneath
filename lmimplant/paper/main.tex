\title{Implant, Conceal, Resurrect:\\ Installing an Exact Circuit in a Real Language Model\\
and Its Fate Under Continued Training}
\author{Gunner Levi Howe\\\texttt{gunnerlevihowe@gmail.com}}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We construct a verified-exact algorithmic circuit --- a one-bit toggle automaton --- inside a single
head of a \emph{trained} linear-RNN language model, and study what continued training does to it.
Two construction facts frame the study. (i)~The dense residual stream of a $30$M-parameter
tied-embedding model has no spare capacity (least-active residual dimension standard deviation
$\liResidMin$ vs.\ median $\liResidMedian$), so an in-place behavioral readout collides with the
model's own RMSNorm and destroys language quality (perplexity rises by $\approx$fourteen orders of
magnitude). (ii)~Adding one inert head (widening $d{=}512{\to}576$, $H{=}8{\to}9$ by zero-padding,
verified identity to $\liWidenInertPct\%$ perplexity) yields a clean substrate: the implanted circuit
reads out behaviorally at $1.00$ accuracy through toggle depth $\liWidenDepth$ at $\liWidenImplantPct\%$
perplexity cost --- an exact skill installed in a real LM, for free. We then continue training under a
pre-registered grid (arms $\{$all-trainable, freeze-implant$\}$ $\times$ exercise mixture
$\{0,10,50\%\}$). The central result: under all-trainable pure-LM training with no exercise,
behavioral toggle decays to chance ($1.00\!\to\!\liBehNoneZeroEnd$) while the internal circuit remains
exact at the end of training ($\liIntNoneZeroEnd$), and re-pinning only the readout interface restores
full behavior ($\liResurrectNoneZero$). The mechanism is \emph{interface drift}: continued training
moves the tied-embedding readout, suppressing behavioral \emph{expression} while the \emph{computation}
persists and is weight-recoverable. A $10\%$ exercise trickle prevents the drift entirely (behavior
stays $1.00$); freezing the circuit's weights but not its interface is \emph{counterproductive}
(internal circuit erodes to $\liIntFreezeZeroEnd$, unrecoverable) because frozen weights cannot
co-adapt to the drifting input; and heavy ($50\%$) exercise diffuses the skill beyond the implanted
head. We position this precisely: it is a ground-truth demonstration, at toy scale, of a phenomenon
the unlearning-robustness literature studies behaviorally --- suppression is not removal --- with the
honest caveats that the circuit is constructed (not learned), the model is a linear RNN (not a
transformer), the skill is trivial, and recovery is white-box. All predictions and kills were
pre-registered before the runs; every number regenerates from committed artifacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Two capabilities the field would like but cannot yet reliably perform: \emph{installing} a specific
computation into a trained model, and \emph{knowing} whether subsequent training removed a computation
or merely hid its expression. The second is a live safety question --- unlearning and safety
fine-tuning are only useful if they \emph{remove} a capability rather than suppressing its surface
behavior \citep{lynch2024eight} --- but it is hard to answer without a ground-truth circuit: when
performance drops, probing cannot tell erasure from concealment.

This paper builds the setting where the question is decidable. We take the verified-exact
state-tracking constructions of our companion work \citep{howe2026automaton} --- solutions written
into DeltaProduct-style Householder linear RNNs and provably correct at initialization --- and use
them as \emph{implants}: an exact circuit inserted by hand into a trained language model, whose
integrity after any amount of further training can be read against a known ceiling; the toy-scale study
of implant fate is our companion \citep{howe2026implant}, which this paper takes to a real LM. Because the delta
rule performs one reflection per token per head, the implantable target is the reflection-length-one
task --- a toggle (lamp on/off) automaton, which is also the trained model's own hardest reliably-%
learned skill in \citet{howe2026hygiene}. We construct it, verify it reads out exactly, continue
training under a pre-registered grid, and measure both its behavioral expression (via the model's
logits) and its internal computation (the implanted head's state, read directly).

\paragraph{What is and is not new.} Compiling exact algorithms into transformer weights is established
--- Tracr \citep{lindner2023tracr} compiles RASP programs into fresh transformers, and InterpBench
\citep{gupta2024interpbench} trains ground-truth circuits into semi-synthetic models. Our construction
is the linear-RNN analog, and is \emph{not} the contribution. The contribution is what happens
\emph{after} insertion into a \emph{trained} model under \emph{continued training}: the concealment /
recovery dynamics, their interface-drift mechanism, and the exercise/freeze dependence. Likewise,
``suppression is not removal'' is a known concern in unlearning \citep{lynch2024eight} and edit
durability \citep{cheng2025erase}; our contribution there is a ground-truth exact-circuit
demonstration and mechanism, not the concept.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An exact circuit installed in a real LM, measured} (\S\ref{sec:construct}): the dense
residual stream forbids a cheap in-place behavioral readout (a construction obstacle we quantify);
one inert added head fixes it, giving exact behavioral readout at $\liWidenImplantPct\%$ perplexity.
\item \textbf{Concealment and recovery under continued training} (\S\ref{sec:battery}): pre-registered;
unexercised pure-LM training suppresses behavior to chance while the circuit stays internally exact and
readout-recoverable.
\item \textbf{The interface-drift mechanism} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): behavior dies because the
tied-embedding readout drifts, not because the computation is destroyed; exercise pins the interface,
and freezing the circuit alone is counterproductive.
\item \textbf{Honest scope and safety framing} (\S\ref{sec:discussion}--\S\ref{sec:limits}): what a
toy, constructed, white-box result does and does not imply for frontier unlearning.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Model.} The trained substrate is the $30$M-parameter DeltaNet-class LM of
\citet{howe2026hygiene} (the arm-A checkpoint; $d{=}512$, $8$ layers, $8$ heads, tied embeddings,
delta rule $S_t{=}(I-\beta_t k_t k_t^\top)S_{t-1}+g_t\beta_t k_t v_t^\top$, $o_t{=}S_t^\top q_t$),
trained on TinyStories \citep{eldan2023tinystories} to perplexity $\liBasePpl$.
\textbf{Implant.} Toggle has reflection length one, so it fits a single head: the lamp lives at one
state entry, a content token sets it, a flip token negates it via the $\beta{=}2$ erase term, a check
token reads it. Exercise uses four dedicated vocabulary tokens (on/off/flip/check); behavioral toggle
is scored from the model's next-token logits, internal toggle from the implanted head's output at the
check position read directly. All predictions/kills were frozen in-repository before any
continued-training run.

\section{Installing the circuit}\label{sec:construct}
\textbf{The dense-stream obstacle.} The toy constructions of \citet{howe2026automaton} live in a clean
dedicated-dimension subspace. A trained tied-embedding LM has none: measured over TinyStories, the
least-active residual dimension has standard deviation $\liResidMin$ against a median of
$\liResidMedian$ (max $\liResidMax$) --- every dimension carries language. Consequently, donating a
head is cheap but head-specific (Fig.~\ref{fig:construct}a: block-$0$ head~$1$ costs $\liHeadOne\%$
perplexity, head~$0$ $\liHeadZero\%$, head~$6$ a catastrophic $\liHeadSix\%$ --- it does
induction-like work), yet routing the circuit's answer to the logits \emph{in place} collides with the
block's output RMSNorm, which amplifies the donated head's near-zero output on quiet real tokens: the
behavioral readout works ($1.00$ toggle) but drives perplexity to $\approx 10^{\liWallLogTen}$. In a
dense residual stream, an exact circuit can be installed and computed but cannot be cheaply wired to
the output.

\textbf{The widened substrate.} Adding one inert $64$-dimensional head ($d{=}512{\to}576$,
$H{=}8{\to}9$) by zero-padding every weight matrix is verified identity: the widened-but-empty model
matches the trained model to $\liWidenInertPct\%$ perplexity. Implanting the toggle circuit into that
fresh head, with the answer on a fresh residual dimension, is then clean --- the new dimensions are
\emph{exactly} zero for real tokens, so the query is exactly zero on real text and the RMSNorm has
nothing to amplify. The result (Fig.~\ref{fig:construct}b): behavioral toggle $1.00$ through depth
$\liWidenDepth$ (well beyond any training length) at $\liWidenImplantPct\%$ perplexity cost. An exact
algorithmic skill now lives in a real language model and the model emits it, at no language cost.

\section{Fate under continued training}\label{sec:battery}
We continue training the implanted widened model for $\liSteps$ steps under a pre-registered
$2\times3$ grid: arm $\in\{$\textbf{none} (all weights trainable), \textbf{freeze-implant} (the
head's implant weights frozen)$\}$ $\times$ exercise mixture $p\in\{0,10,50\%\}$ of dedicated-token
toggle sequences vs.\ pure TinyStories. We log behavioral and internal toggle and perplexity every
$300$ steps, and after training re-pin the readout interface (the answer-routing weight and the
on/off embedding rows) and re-measure behavior (the resurrection probe). Table~\ref{tab:battery} and
Figs.~\ref{fig:traj}--\ref{fig:tax} give the results.

\textbf{Pre-registered verdicts.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{IL1 (unexercised erosion): confirmed.} none/$0\%$ behavioral toggle decays
$1.00\to\liBehNoneZeroEnd$ ($\le0.60$).
\item \textbf{IL2 (exercise protects): confirmed.} none end-behavior is nondecreasing in exercise
($\liBehNoneZeroEnd, \liBehNoneTenEnd, \liBehNoneFiftyEnd$); a $10\%$ trickle holds it at $1.00$
throughout.
\item \textbf{IL3 (freeze preserves): not confirmed --- and the failure is a finding.} freeze/$0\%$
dies to $\liBehFreezeZeroEnd$ and does not recover ($\liResurrectFreezeZero$), \emph{worse} than the
all-trainable none/$0\%$ (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}).
\item \textbf{IL4 (resurrection): confirmed where applicable.} Every cell whose behavior decayed while
the internal circuit stayed intact recovers to $1.00$ by re-pinning the readout (none/$0\%$:
behavioral $\liBehNoneZeroEnd$, internal $\liIntNoneZeroEnd$, resurrection $\liResurrectNoneZero$).
The one genuinely eroded cell (freeze/$0\%$: internal $\liIntFreezeZeroEnd$) correctly does not recover
($\liResurrectFreezeZero$): the internal read separates concealed from eroded.
\item \textbf{Kills.} KL2 (implant unpreservable everywhere) does not fire (none/$10\%$ fully
preserves). KL3 (exercise collapses perplexity $>{+}10\%$) technically fires at $p{=}50\%$
($+\liPplPctNoneFifty\%$) but is confounded: the no-exercise none/$0\%$ cell already drifts
$+\liDriftFloorPct\%$ from continued training alone, so most of the rise is generic optimization drift,
not exercise (\S\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The mechanism: interface drift}\label{sec:mechanism}
Why does an unexercised implant lose its behavior but not its computation? The vulnerable object is
the \emph{interface}, not the circuit. The dedicated tokens' embeddings and the answer-readout weights
are tied into the model's shared embedding and receive gradients on every step (the tied embedding is
also the output head), so continued training drifts them even when the toggle task never appears in
the data. Behavioral toggle therefore decays (Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}b) while the implanted head's
\emph{computation}, read directly, survives.

Two arms confirm the mechanism. \textbf{Exercise pins the interface:} at $p\ge10\%$ the dedicated
tokens appear in training, their embeddings stay put, and behavior never drifts (IL2). \textbf{Freezing
the circuit is counterproductive:} in none/$0\%$ the trainable circuit \emph{co-adapts} to the drifting
input and its internal integrity re-forms to exact by the end ($\liIntNoneZeroEnd$); in freeze/$0\%$
the frozen circuit cannot compensate for the drifting input embeddings, so its internal computation
breaks ($\liIntFreezeZeroEnd$) and is unrecoverable. Preserving an implant thus requires pinning its
\emph{input/output interface}, not its weights --- the opposite of the naive intervention.

A third, unpredicted phenomenon: at heavy exercise ($p{=}50\%$) behavioral toggle stays $1.00$ while
the \emph{single-head} internal read drops ($\liIntNoneFiftyEnd$). Heavy practice diffuses the skill
beyond the implanted head into redundant routes, so a one-head read no longer reflects behavior. The
implant seeds a capability the surrounding model then distributes.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The clean statement of the result: continued training can drive a deliberately-installed capability's
behavioral \emph{expression} to chance while its underlying \emph{computation} persists intact and is
recoverable by re-aligning the readout --- and a ground-truth internal read distinguishes this
concealment from genuine erasure, which the same battery also produces. This is the mechanistic,
provable version of the unlearning-robustness observation that suppression need not be removal
\citep{lynch2024eight}: here we can \emph{prove} the mechanism survived because we built it exactly,
and we identify \emph{why} (interface drift) and \emph{what prevents preservation} (freezing weights
without the interface).

We are deliberately careful about reach. The safety-relevant reading --- ``training may hide rather
than remove capabilities'' --- is supported here only in a specific, constructed, white-box setting,
and the recovery mechanism is interface-specific. What the result contributes to that conversation is
a ground-truth existence proof and a named mechanism, not evidence that natural capabilities in
frontier models behave this way. Section~\ref{sec:limits} states the gaps that would have to close
before the reading generalizes.

\section{Limitations and honest scope}\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Constructed, not learned.} The implant is a hand-built exact circuit; a naturally-trained
capability may drift differently. This is the single largest gap to any frontier claim.
\textbf{Linear RNN, not transformer.} The clean single-head delta-rule construction and the widening
trick are architecture-specific; the transformer analog (Tracr-style) is a different, harder
construction. \textbf{Trivial skill.} Toggle is one bit ($\mathbb{Z}_2$); it is not a capability
anyone would care to hide, and higher-reflection-length skills need multi-head constructions (open).
\textbf{White-box recovery.} ``Resurrection'' re-pins weights we saved; it is not an adversary
recovering a suppressed capability from a released checkpoint. \textbf{Dedicated-token protocol.} The
skill is exercised through reserved tokens, not natural language; natural-language triggers are
untested. \textbf{Single seed; perplexity drift.} One seed; and the continued-training setup drifts
perplexity $+\liDriftFloorPct\%$ even at zero exercise (learning rate not tuned for minimal-drift
continuation), which does not affect the within-run toggle-integrity findings (all relative to each
run's own start) but means the absolute perplexity numbers are not a clean cost measurement.
\textbf{The freeze arm's confound is reported as a finding}, not corrected. None of these is hidden;
together they place the work as a toy-scale mechanistic study, and the path to relevance is to close
at least two of {constructed$\to$learned, RNN$\to$transformer, white-box$\to$realistic-recovery,
trivial$\to$meaningful}.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}
All code, the pre-registration (frozen before the battery), the $6$ cell artifacts, the fact-capture
script (\texttt{capture\_facts.py}, which regenerates every construction/feasibility number into
\texttt{facts.json}), and this paper's number/figure pipeline are published in the \texttt{lmimplant/}
directory of \url{https://github.com/gunnerhowe/automaton-underneath}. Every number here is a macro
generated by \texttt{gen\_numbers.py} from \texttt{facts.json} and \texttt{runs\_wide/*.json};
\texttt{verify\_regen.py} re-derives them and byte-compares before release. Compute:
$\approx2.5$ GPU-hours (battery) plus construction probes on one RTX 3080.
